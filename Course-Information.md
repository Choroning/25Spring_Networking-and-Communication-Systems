## 2. Schedule and Class Format

- **Department:** Department of Autonomous Mobility
- **Shared Course:** The course is offered as a shared course, so there are syllabi under two codes, `DAMO423-00` and `DSC-211012`. The `DSC-211012` syllabus (a competency-based syllabus) lists the course category as major elective (211012), the operating university as Korea University Sejong Campus, the operating department as the Autonomous Driving Systems major, and the English course title as Networking and Communication Systems.
- **Type:** Major elective
- **Credits:** 3 (credits/theory/lab in the `DSC-211012` syllabus: 3/3/0)
- **Class Time:** Saturday period 2
- **Classroom:** Accelerator ICT Convergence Building, Room 324
- **Class Format:** The course ran as an online MOOC of the DSC shared university (Daejeon-Sejong-Chungnam Regional Innovation Platform), and each week's video lectures, divided into three sessions, were taken on the LMS operated by Chungnam National University.
  - The activity types are lectures and quizzes.
  - The `DSC-211012` syllabus allocates the class methods as 90% lectures and 10% experiments and lab exercises.
- **Interaction Plan:** The instructor and students interact through weekly monitoring, individual guidance and meetings, email, and mentoring when needed, and students interact with each other through discussion and sharing of materials (`DSC-211012` syllabus).
- **Course Description:** This course builds an understanding of wired communication and of wireless communication linked with C-ITS, which are essential to most jobs related to autonomous vehicles, such as control, sensor processing, and software development. Students study the need for, types, connection structures, and use of wired communication for autonomous vehicles as a whole, with a particular focus on how to use CAN communication, the most fundamental wired communication in vehicles. They study the concepts and types of wireless communication for autonomous vehicles, such as V2I between autonomous vehicles and infrastructure and V2V between vehicles, the concepts and use cases of cooperative automated driving systems linked with C-ITS infrastructure, and the direction of development in future mobility. The course also introduces the major automotive research institutes of Korea, Japan, the United Kingdom, and France. It includes basic material so that students without background knowledge of communication can take it, and some background knowledge for understanding the characteristics of the automotive industry, which students aiming for the automotive field need.
- **Course Goals:** The `DSC-211012` syllabus states that students study in-vehicle communication and communication between the vehicle and the outside world as a whole, centered on autonomous driving, and sets the course goals as follows.
  1. Understand the need for, types, and composition of wired and wireless communication systems for autonomous vehicles.
  2. Understand wired in-vehicle communication networks such as CAN, FlexRay, and Ethernet and their application to autonomous driving system architectures.
  3. Understand wireless communication technologies for vehicles such as WAVE, C-V2X, and CALM.
  4. Understand the composition and direction of development of cooperative automated driving and C-ITS systems.
- **Learning Goals:**
  1. Acquire basic knowledge of networks and communication, and study the in-vehicle networks LIN, CAN, FlexRay, and Ethernet. Also study the concepts of the vehicular wireless communication networks WAVE, C-V2X, and DSRC and of CALM, the standard that links them.
  2. Acquire knowledge of how wired and wireless networks are applied to vehicles and why they are needed. In particular, study the use of wired and wireless networks in the architecture of autonomous driving systems, and examine the situation in each country regarding how vehicle application services built on them are developing.
- **Core Competency Weights in the `DSC-211012` Syllabus:** 25% ICT skill application, 35% systems thinking, 10% project execution, 10% convergent problem-solving, 10% creative innovation, and 10% career learning. Technical communication, community contribution, and aesthetic sensibility are 0%.
- **Competency-Based Learning Goals:** Learning goal 1 corresponds to ICT skill application, and learning goal 2 to systems thinking. Project execution corresponds to the goal of examining the technical understanding and use of the CAN network, the most common and important wired network in vehicles, and convergent problem-solving corresponds to the goal of covering the functions that can be implemented in autonomous vehicles by using wired and wireless networks.
- **Main Core Competency and Links to Other Courses:** Students build the competency in wired and wireless networks that is essential for implementing autonomous driving systems in vehicles. The course includes basic material on communication and networking so that students without background knowledge of communication can take it, and it includes basic, advanced, and hands-on material on CAN communication, regarded as the most frequently used and most important of the vehicle communication technologies.
- **Prerequisites:** There is no recommended prerequisite or enrollment requirement.

- Weeks 8 (midterm exam) and 15 (final exam) have no lecture note.
